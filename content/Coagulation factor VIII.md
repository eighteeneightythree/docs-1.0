Factor VIII, or antihaemophilic factor (A), is plasma glycoprotein that circulates in a complex with von Willebrand factor. It is about 330kDa.

Its primary site of production is not clear; it is synthesised by endothelial cells of the liver and other organs.
Congenital (or acquired) deficiency of FVIII is the cause of haemophilia A.

In liver disease, FVIII level are often paradoxically elevated, which can produce a normal [[Coagulation studies#Activated partial thromboplastin time|aPTT]]. Severe hepatosis may increase the aPTT as the liver sinusoidal endothelial cells become involved.

Liver transplantation has been noted to 'cure' haemophilia by restoring production of FVIII[^1].
