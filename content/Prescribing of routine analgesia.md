Routine analgesia is typically simple analgesia with the addition of opiate analgesia as required. Most analgesic regimens are built on the "pain ladder". The below are doses for standard immediate release formulation[^1].

# Simple analgesia
## Paracetamol
> [!none]- 1000mg *quaque 4-6 hora*, max 4g in 24 hours
> For body weights under 50: 15mg/kg, not exceeding 60mg/kg or 3g

## Ibuprofen
> [!none] 400mg *3-4 in die*, max 2.4g in 24 hours
> 

## Celecoxib
Selective NSAIDs are as effective for the treatment of acute pain as their nonselective counterparts. Their primary benefit is in their minimal action against COX-1 and thus their more favourable side effect profile and reduced platelet effects. They are probably not better than ibuprofen unless COX-1 inhibition is likely to cause issues.
>[!none]- 200mg once daily
>up to 400mg may be given daily in 1 or 2 divided doses if required, max 400mg in 24 hours.

# Opioids

Opiate analgesia requires different dosing based on route.

# Weak opioids

## Tramadol
> [!none] 50-100mg not more than *quaque 4 hora*, max ~400mg daily

## Codeine
>[!none]- 30-60mg *quaque 4 hora* as required, max 240mg in 24 hours
>Sometimes 15mg is good enough

# Strong opioids

## Morphine
>[!none]- ORAL: 5-10mg *quaque 4 hora*, adjusted according to response
>Doses up to 30mg are probably reasonable...
>It is recommended to specify brand when prescribing oral morphine. 


>[!none]- IV: load 2-5mg (slowly); then *quaque 4 hora* adjusted according to response.
>Reduce dose in the elderly.
>In the acute setting, a 10mL syringe can be made up to 1mg/mL and used for initial control of severe pain. 

## Oxycodone
>[!none] ORAL: 5mg *quaque 4-6 hora*, adjusted according to response

## Fentanyl
> [!none]- IV: load 1-2mcg/kg (25-100mcg); then 0.35-0.5mcg/kg (25-50mcg) *quaque hora* 
> Fentanyl use is probably best left in ED or for critically ill patients.
