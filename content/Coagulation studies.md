A routine coagulation test run by [[The medical laboratory|Awanui labs]] includes the following component tests:
 - [[#Prothrombin time (9-13s)|PT]]
 - [[#Activated partial thromboplastin time (20-31s)|aPTT]]
 - [[#Fibrinogen (1.7-4.3g/L)|Fibrinogen]] 
 - +/- [[#International normalised ratio|INR]]

# [[Prothrombin time]] (9-13s)

The prothrombin time is a measure of [[Coagulation cascade#extrinsic pathway|extrinsic pathway]] activity.

## Causes of prolonged PT
- [[#International normalised ratio|Warfarin (and other vitamin K agonists)]]
- Vitamin K deficiency
- Hepatosis
- [[Disseminated intravascular coagulation|DIC]]

# [[Activated partial thromboplastin time]] (20-31s)
The aPTT is a measure of [[Coagulation cascade#intrinsic pathway|intrinsic pathway]] activity.

## Causes of prolonged aPTT
- Haemophilia A, B, or C
- von Willebrand disease
- Heparin
- Severe hepatosis ([[Coagulation factor VIII|see FVIII]])
- DIC
- Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) causes a paradoxical increase in aPTT *in vitro* despite producing a hypercoagulable state*

# [[Fibrinogen]] (1.7-4.3g/L)

 Hypofibrinogenaemia is either consumptive or due to a production deficit:
 - DIC
 - Liver disease
 - Transfusion

Hyperfibrinogenaemia may be an acute phase response:
- Inflammation
- Pregnancy
- Cancer
- Trauma

Fibrinogen concentration is typically tested for with immunoassays or the Clauss fibrinogen assay. 

# [[International normalised ratio]]
The INR is a standardised measure of [[Coagulation studies#Prothrombin time (9-13s)|PT]]. 
The normal INR is 0.8-1.2.
The therapeutic range in 2.0-3.0.
